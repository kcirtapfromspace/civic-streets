**Curbwise UX audit · September 27, 2026**

**Verdict: the product has a credible civic direction, but it does not yet meet a high standard for an end-to-end proposal service.** The strongest work is the newer Concern → Explore → Brief journey. The largest weaknesses are continuity, recipient usefulness, and inconsistent behavior between entry points. These are consequential product issues even though the automated application checks pass.

The distinctive quality to pursue is care: preserving someone's words, making uncertain information legible, respecting their time, explaining tradeoffs, and helping another person respond. The current restrained typography and original street illustration are appropriate. A more decorative visual treatment would leave the principal weaknesses intact.

**Scope and evidence.** This is a heuristic audit supported by source review and browser walkthroughs, not a usability study or accessibility certification. I reviewed the landing, navigation, map controls, observation capture/detail, proposal wizard, saved drafts, standalone street editor, intersection flow, representative draft, PDF export, account context, shared controls, and analytics. Browser work used an isolated local Vite instance under Node 24 with Convex and PostHog disabled. Observations and recipients entered for the audit were local examples; no public reports, emails, or city requests were sent. Map/provider reads were exercised, but connected submission, account recovery, paid entitlements, and institutional operations were assessed from code where relevant, not certified in production.

Desktop was inspected at 1440 × 1000; mobile at 390 × 844, with an additional 320 × 700 editor check. Two actual UI-generated PDFs were downloaded and every page rendered and inspected. Axe-core 4.11.0 sampled three workflow states using WCAG A/AA rule tags. Manual screen-reader testing, real mobile keyboards, production performance, photo-heavy/maximum-length PDFs, multilingual use, and recipient field research remain unmeasured.

The newer proposal carries original observation context forward and distinguishes it from editable concern text. Existing dimensions are explicitly assumed, estimated, or contributor-measured. Completed map drafts survive reload; this was verified, including preservation of the requested next step. Exports retain a reusable download link. **Proposed workflow.** The durable object is a proposal workspace that can contain only a concern, or several design alternatives. Its record should not require geometry to exist. “Small” and “large” describe how much evidence and exploration the task needs, not different standards of respect or separate apps.

| Stage | Small change, such as an obstructed ramp | Larger change, such as a corridor redesign | Durable output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Locate | Address/intersection, side/corner, optional photo | Segment extent, crossings, boundaries | Place with uncertainty recorded |
| Describe | What happens, who is affected, when noticed | Repeated conditions, affected activities, local goals | Resident's original concern and optional evidence |
| Choose next step | Inspection, maintenance request, conversation | Site walk, study, feedback on alternatives, possible authorized pilot | Specific ask and intended audience |
| Explore, if useful | Photo annotation or one modest intervention | Baseline plus named alternatives, assumptions and tradeoffs | Options linked to the same concern |
| Review and share | One-page brief, plain text, optional published link | Summary brief plus optional evidence/technical appendix | Readable, versioned artifact with deliberate visibility |
| Return | Record response, confirm condition, revise | Resolve questions, incorporate feedback, retain decisions | History and next action with truthful status |

The workspace should keep the original observation distinct from the proposed interpretation, keep measurements distinct from assumptions, and keep self-reported follow-up distinct from verified agency events. A practical initial data model needs optional geometry, purpose, evidence references, visibility, save state, and revisions. Structured alternatives and response history can be added incrementally. This is a direction for a focused product consolidation, not authorization to migrate the schema in this audit.

**Artifact standards.** A useful brief lets a reader answer five questions quickly: Where is this? What is happening and to whom? What evidence supports it? What is being proposed or requested? What remains uncertain? The reader should then know how to respond.

| Artifact | Minimum useful content | Readiness check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observation note | Place, observed condition, observation date/uncertainty, supporting photo/text | A reader can distinguish a dated report from a verified current condition. |
| Small-request brief | Concern, requested next step, relevant evidence, optional contributor contact | Reader can route or discuss it without asking what action is requested. |
| Option comparison | Same baseline, named alternatives, spatial changes, affected activities, assumptions, open questions | Gains and losses are equally visible; wider claims are not inferred from a drawing. |
| Meeting brief | Short summary, locality map where useful, selected option or question, evidence sources, revision | Works printed, on a phone, and without the app. |
| Contact draft | Accurate recipient, resident voice, concrete ask, working link or manual attachment steps | Contains the substance of the brief; no phantom link, attachment, or delivery claim. |
| Follow-up record | What was shared, who replied, what changed, next action, verification status | Supports continued work rather than manufacturing a completed outcome. |

| --- | --- | --- |
| Understand the service | At least 8 of 10 first-time participants can explain what is saved/shared and whether the city receives it | Ask after the landing and again at handoff; any false belief about actual submission triggers a copy/workflow fix. |
| Small-request completion | At least 8 of 10 finish a useful brief unaided within 5 minutes | Moderated representative tasks; exclude time needed to obtain a real field photo, but record that burden separately. |
| Larger-concept completion | At least 8 of 10 create and explain an option unaided within 15 minutes | Require correct recognition of assumptions and one gain/loss, not just pressing export. |
| Recipient comprehension | At least 4 of 5 intended recipients can state place, problem, ask, and an actionable next step within 60 seconds | Blind review of realistic briefs without help from the author. |
| Continuity | Zero lost-work cases in the agreed interruption matrix | Reload/navigation at every stage, failed save, storage quota, reopened draft, failed export, and context switching. |
| Artifact consistency | Every handoff preserves the chosen concern, request, location, evidence references, and revision | Contract tests across HTML/PDF/email plus visual inspection of short, long, and photo-heavy fixtures. |
| Honest claims | Zero false saved/sent/attached/approved states in tested paths | Fault injection and copy review, including empty/failed validation and mail-client failure. |
| Accessibility | No known A/AA blockers in core tasks; zero critical/serious automated findings in sampled release states | Automated scans plus keyboard, VoiceOver/Safari and NVDA/Firefox or Chrome completion, 200%/400% zoom and 320 CSS-pixel reflow. |
| Touch/readability | Prefer 44px primary touch controls; all applicable AA target/contrast rules met | Include dense editor rows and drawers, not only shared buttons. |
| Field performance | p75 LCP ≤2.5s, INP ≤200ms, CLS ≤0.1 by device class | Production field data, supplemented by throttled lab tests; separately measure usable map and first saved draft. |
| Continued civic use | Establish baseline before choosing a target | Consented pilot follow-up: artifact used in a real conversation, recipient response, revision, or documented next action. |

The performance thresholds come from [Core Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals). This audit did not measure them. The build emitted large-chunk advisories, including roughly 274KB gzip for the map-related `pin-utils` chunk and 514KB gzip for the lazy PDF renderer; sizes alone do not prove a poor user experience. Reflow and focus checks should follow the actual [W3C reflow criterion](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html) and [focus-not-obscured criterion](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html), accounting for genuinely two-dimensional content without exempting the surrounding form.

**Research plan.** Run two small formative rounds before broad expansion. Recruit residents with a real concern, neighborhood organizers, and intended recipients such as planners or maintenance/service staff. Include people who use assistive technology, older phones, limited technical vocabulary, and different levels of civic experience. Compensate participation and use consented examples. A small round identifies problems; it does not establish a statistically stable conversion rate.

Give participants concrete tasks: document a blocked ramp; propose a safer crossing; compare a modest intervention with a larger street option; resume after interruption; explain whether a request was sent. Give recipients the resulting artifact without the maker present and ask what they understand, what they would do, and what is missing. Capture task completion, help required, time, critical misunderstandings, trust failures, and the participant's own language. Do not turn approval of a mockup into evidence that an agency will act.

**Instrumentation.** Existing analytics emphasize submissions, saves, exports, votes, and mail-draft opening. They miss much of the funnel, while intersection “completed” fires on reset. Define started, stage reached, save succeeded/failed, resumed, export prepared/download requested, handoff chosen, and user-reported response events. Keep concern text, addresses, photos, contact details, and raw error contents out of analytics. Measure stage conversion and failure recovery by proposal type and device class. Browser download and mailto actions are not proof that an artifact was read or a message delivered. Measure actual downstream use through deliberate feedback or consented pilot follow-up.

**Recommended delivery sequence.** Finish a complete, dependable vertical slice before adding another creation mode.

| Order | Work package | Exit condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Repair trust and loss: findings 1–8; actionable photo rule | Every supported path retains work and produces a truthful artifact; core accessibility blockers removed. |
| 2 | Unify concern, private draft, basic export, and My work | A small request and a larger concept share one durable identity and can resume at any stage. |
| 3 | Improve decision quality and artifact provenance | Options show tradeoffs; evidence and uncertainty survive export; recipients can identify the ask. |
| 4 | Consolidate mobile layout and component tokens | Same action vocabulary, primary control behavior, contrast and focus quality across all workflows. |
| 5 | Validate with residents and recipients; add lightweight follow-up | Observed task/recipient results meet the agreed targets; documented learning informs the next release. |

Suggested first release scope: one pilot-area concern → private draft → optional single concept → accessible brief → recipient handoff → return-to-work journey. Preserve exploratory tools, but avoid presenting incomplete branches as equivalent finished workflows. Restore the intersection journey to that same completion contract before expanding it.
