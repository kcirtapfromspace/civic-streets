## 1. Denver DOTI/CALC Micro-Grant — immediate lead

### Amount, status, applicant, portal

The official [2026 application hub](https://denver-doti.submittable.com/submit) states applications are open, reviewed throughout 2026 until funds are exhausted. Individuals can request $500 project funding, followed by $75 after completion/reflection. Organizations and businesses with a valid W-9 can request $1,500, followed by $500 after completion/reflection. Projects must primarily benefit Denver residents. This is an individual/business route without a stated nonprofit requirement.

[Direct 2026 application](https://denver-doti.submittable.com/submit/356578/2026-doti-calc-micro-grant-program). A free Submittable account is required. Detailed questions are behind sign-in; exact field wording, character limits, and attachment count remain unverified. Prepare an editable narrative, rather than claiming to have completed the live application.

### Critical project rules

The user's TEST URL is superseded by the [canonical city program page](https://denvergov.org/Government/Agencies-Departments-Offices/Agencies-Departments-Offices-Directory/Department-of-Transportation-and-Infrastructure/Programs-Services/OCBE#section-4), also reached through [DOTIMicroGrants](https://www.denvergov.org/DOTIMicroGrants).

- Walk/bike audits and mobility education qualify. No indirect costs or business-only benefit.
- Historically funds run out mid–late September; one funded proposal per applicant/year.
- Apply 30 days ahead; 60 if permits needed. Eligibility form precedes proposal.
- All projects need insurance before payment; insurance may be budgeted. CGL: $1 million/occurrence; Denver additional insured. Workers compensation as applicable.
- Prepare location permission, COI, safety/consent/privacy plans; business signed W-9/address matching Secretary of State. Additional permits depend on risk/location.
- Project funds follow approved documents and grant-officer consultation; complete within 90 days of first payment. Reflection: within two weeks afterward; track participation, outcomes, anecdotes, photos.
- Direct project costs are allowed; software/hosting is not specifically named. Seek a ruling before budgeting these costs.
- Contact: communitystreets@denvergov.org.

### Source discrepancy

The separate [CALC microgrants marketing page](https://www.denvercalc.org/microgrants) still says closed/open soon for 2026. The city page and live 2026 application hub both say open. Use those as the stronger status evidence while explicitly checking fund availability.

### Recommended framing

**Proposed project:** a Denver neighborhood walk audit, accessible workshop, resident comparison of street concepts, and a shareable summary of concerns and priorities. Select the neighborhood with actual residents and a host. Budget direct delivery costs; track separately any proposed Curbwise usage costs pending approval. Do not treat the additional completion payment as cash available to run the event.

## 2. The Denver Foundation — Strengthening Neighborhoods

**Status:** Open through October 19, 2026; rolling review with monthly decisions. **Amount:** $500–$5,000; average $2,500. **Applicant:** 501(c)(3) or group with a 501(c)(3) fiscal sponsor, led by at least three unrelated people, located and working in the seven-county Metro Denver region. The current rules no longer accept unsponsored groups. Supports grassroots campaigns/projects or capacity-building consultants, prioritizing communities below area median income and communities of color. Participation supports such as interpretation and childcare fit its examples; retroactive funding is excluded. [Official guidelines, Spanish version](https://denverfoundation.org/funding-opportunity/strengthening-neighborhoods/?lang=es) (English endpoint intermittently blocked during verification).

**Apply:** [TDF Grant Manager — Strengthening Neighborhoods and DESCI](https://www.grantinterface.com/Home/Logon?urlkey=denver). Contact sn@denverfoundation.org. This is a separate portal from the foundation's other competitive funds. The foundation's [opportunity directory](https://denverfoundation.org/funding-opportunities/) says application deadlines are 5 p.m. Mountain.

**Our fit assessment:** promising for a resident steering group using Curbwise to organize around safer walking, rolling, biking, and transit. The work and budget must be community-led; a founder-only software request would be weak. First confirm the three leaders and fiscal sponsor before investing in the complete application.

## 3. Colorado Health Foundation — Building and Bridging Connections

**Status:** Rolling, **invitation required after a program-officer conversation**. **Amount/term:** typically $20,000–$45,000 for 12–24 months; decisions generally within 45 days of submission. Supports community relationships and collective action addressing health equity. Must identify a specific geographic community and show existing trust, shared roles, and community support. Informal community groups with fiscal sponsors and local public agencies are contemplated. It explicitly excludes projects whose applicant is entering a new community through planning, assessments, surveys, or education without authentic relationships. [Official program](https://coloradohealth.org/funding-opportunities/building-and-bridging-connections-funding).

**Route:** Use the program page's “Find a Program Officer” link, discuss fit, obtain invitation, then apply through the foundation's grants portal. Its [rolling-funding directory](https://coloradohealth.org/priorities/rolling-funding) independently confirms this invitation requirement.

**Our fit assessment:** plausible after the pilot, with an established neighborhood nonprofit and resident leaders owning a sustained program. Use Curbwise within that community's chosen work. This is not an immediately available startup grant or a reason to invent pre-existing relationships.

### Colorado Health Foundation applicant structure

The [foundation FAQ](https://coloradohealth.org/frequently-asked-questions) prioritizes Colorado-serving nonprofits and public agencies; a tax-exempt fiscal sponsor may apply for another group. Sponsor-board approval must precede submission. Individuals, for-profit organizations, and programs operated by or for for-profit benefit are generally excluded unless an opportunity states otherwise. Do not assume an LLC becomes eligible merely by asking a nonprofit to pass through funds. Any vendor role or commercial software expense needs a legitimate charitable project and funder/sponsor approval.

## 4. Colorado Health Foundation — Rapid Response: Advocacy

**Status:** Open rolling per its [current rolling-funding directory](https://coloradohealth.org/priorities/rolling-funding); no fixed deadline. **Amount/term:** generally $15,000–$75,000 for 3–9 months; usual decision within 45 days. Requires a real, time-sensitive public-policy opportunity and community backing. Safe daily physical activity for children is an explicit priority. Eligible tactics include organizing, coalition building, policy analysis, public/policymaker education and messaging. It excludes bridge funding, previously planned initiatives, long-term projects, partisan activity, and behavior-only/direct-service projects. Applicant structure follows the foundation FAQ above. [Official program and application route](https://coloradohealth.org/funding-opportunities/rapid-response-advocacy).

**Our fit assessment:** reserve for a nonprofit-led response to an actual upcoming transportation policy decision. It cannot honestly be pitched as funding an already planned general site launch. Register in the foundation's portal about a week before submission. An older “recent funding” index also lists a similarly named program as closed; the current rolling directory explicitly identifies this live route as open.

## 5. The Denver Foundation — Civic Fabric Fund, State & Local Policy

**Status:** Public page says open August 3–September 15, 2026, but **invitation only**. **Amount:** $15,000–$30,000, potentially general operating or program support. Applicant must be Colorado-based/serving, a 501(c)(3) or fiscally sponsored; coalitions can apply through one lead. Organizations receiving a Community Grants Program award in the same calendar year are ineligible. Funds policy/advocacy, including research, community engagement and coalition building aligned with foundation policy principles. [Official guidelines and portal](https://denverfoundation.org/funding-opportunity/civic-fabric-fund-state-local-policy/). Contact: Naomi Amaha, namaha@denverfoundation.org.

**Our fit assessment:** future relationship-building through an eligible advocacy partner. Do not label the September 15 deadline as an available unsolicited application or rush a speculative submission.
