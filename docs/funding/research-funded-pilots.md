# Funded pilots and startup R&D — broader geography

Verified September 14, 2026. No contacts, accounts, applications, or purchases were made. This search expands beyond Colorado and distinguishes grants, contracts, and investment.

## Finding

**An open program is not the same as a ready source of cash for Curbwise.** Four live U.S. startup-funding routes below warrant a fit check; one live international paid-development competition requires a substantial product pivot. None is verified as an immediate, unrestricted payment for the current website. Moving the pilot alone does not satisfy programs that require the business and its leadership to be based in the state.

My recommendation is to pursue customer-funded pilots with the current product while checking NSF technical fit. Consider Maine or Massachusetts only if genuine business operations there already make strategic sense. Do not relocate or change the product just to pursue an unawarded grant.

## 1. NSF SBIR Phase I — national R&D grant

**Live route:** submit a Project Pitch; a full Phase I proposal requires invitation. Next full deadline: **November 4, 2026, 5 p.m. applicant local time**. Up to **$305,000 for 6–18 months**, no equity; committed voluntary cost sharing is prohibited. Eligible small-business concerns have at most 500 employees including affiliates. The principal investigator must primarily work for the company during the award. SBIR does not require a research partner; STTR does. [Current NSF 26-510 solicitation](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/small-business-innovation-research-small-business-technology/nsf26-510/solicitation).

**Apply:** [Project Pitch](https://seedfund.nsf.gov/apply/project-pitch/), then invited proposal via Research.gov. The pitch covers technical innovation, research challenges, market, and team; NSF states a typical 1–2 month pitch response. Starting now does not guarantee an invitation in time for November. [Full proposal instructions and registrations](https://seedfund.nsf.gov/apply/full-proposal/).

**Fit:** conditional. Curbwise needs a defensible technical research problem—such as a genuinely new, testable method for recovering and validating street geometry under uncertainty—not merely connecting existing AI services, adding features, hosting, or customer acquisition. This example is a hypothesis to assess, not a claim of existing novelty. Establish technical risk and competitive differentiation before investing in the full proposal.

**Cash timing:** grant after award, not customer revenue. Exact award-specific drawdown terms were not verified here; do not assume the full amount is deposited upfront. U.S. small-business ownership, work-location and registration requirements require a complete eligibility screen.

## 2. Maine Technology Institute — Business Innovation Funding

**Live route:** rolling; first awards typically **$10,000–$30,000**, cumulative grants up to $100,000. Loans/equity are separate possible instruments; request the grant route explicitly. Supports innovative products/services, R&D, prototyping and commercialization. Match is at least **1:1**; cash must constitute at least 50% of match up to $15,000, 70% at $15,000–$30,000, increasing at higher cumulative levels. A working prototype is not required at the lowest tiers. [Official FAQ](https://www.mainetechnology.org/explore-funding-programs/funding-faqs/).

**Location and cash:** regular Maine business location plus a qualifying executive/founder living in Maine full-time. Out-of-state applicants can propose establishing that presence, but funding waits for substantial location progress. Review typically takes about two months; milestone/conditional payments are possible. Costs before contacting MTI and starting the process are not reimbursed. [FAQ](https://www.mainetechnology.org/explore-funding-programs/funding-faqs/).

**Apply:** [Business Innovation Funding](https://www.mainetechnology.org/explore-funding-programs/business-innovation-funding/) → Startup Questionnaire → staff discussion. No municipality partner prerequisite is stated for this general business route.

**Fit:** potentially more flexible than research-only grants, if Curbwise proves novel commercial value and Maine economic impact. A short visit or remote pilot in Portland would not meet the leadership-location requirement. Match and relocation make this unsuitable for a founder with no working capital or Maine plans.

## 3. MassCEC Catalyst — early prototype climate-tech grant

**Live route:** up to **$75,000**; **September 30, 2026, 5 p.m. Eastern**. Questions due September 15. Award decisions expected late January 2027. Supports climate technologies at TRL 2–4 (concept through laboratory validation). [Program and current application links](https://www.masscec.com/program/catalyst).

**Applicant:** Massachusetts-based early-stage climate-tech company, at most four full-time-equivalent employees and no more than $2.5 million combined financing/grants/revenue over five years. RFP requires headquarters and primary operating functions in Massachusetts. A pre-incorporation team can apply, but must incorporate to receive funds. This is a grant, not equity investment. A numeric match requirement was not identified; do not invent one. Cash follows executed agreement and documentation, potentially milestone installments; the sample agreement specifies three installments. Pre-agreement costs cannot be charged. [Fall 2026 RFP, eligibility and contract](https://www.masscec.com/sites/default/files/documents/FY27_Fall_-_Catalyst_-_RFP_-_Final_081426.pdf).

**Fit:** conditional and probably premature to pursue without established Massachusetts operations and demonstrable climate-tech novelty. Quantify the plausible climate mechanism; an attractive street rendering alone is not an emissions result. This is a research-prototype route, not immediate city-pilot revenue.

## 4. MassCEC InnovateMass — demonstration grant

**Live route:** up to **$350,000**, **October 20, 2026, 11:59 p.m. Eastern**. Applicant team needs a Massachusetts-based climate-tech company and committed demonstration partner(s). Software is eligible. Public schedule expects awards around January/February and **projects beginning summer 2027**. [Program](https://www.masscec.com/program/innovatemass).

**Requirements:** proprietary climate technology at TRL 5–8; U.S. company plus Massachusetts operating presence, with signed partner commitments. Cost share equals at least **50% of grant**, reduced to **25% at qualifying Public Benefit Sites**; at least 20% of the required share must be cash. Payments follow achieved milestones, accepted deliverables, invoices, proof of expenditure and cost-share documentation. No payment before an executed agreement. The RFP allows service providers/subcontractors on applicant teams, creating a possible vendor role through a qualified lead. [September 2026 RFP](https://www.masscec.com/sites/default/files/documents/FY27_Fall_-_InnovateMass_-_RFP.pdf).

**Fit:** longer-term, not fast cash. Stronger if a qualified Massachusetts lead and local deployment host need Curbwise's technology for a measured decarbonization/adaptation demonstration. A service role must be necessary and budgeted; it is not a workaround for lead eligibility. Catalyst and InnovateMass serve different development stages, and cannot both fund the same work.

## 5. CivTech Scotland Round 12 — paid development contract, major pivot

**Live competition:** closes **October 20, 2026, noon Scotland time** via Public Contracts Scotland. Challenge 12.4 concerns NHS patient transport, initially renal care, with exploration January 11–29 and accelerator March 1–June 11, 2027. Advertised **£650,000 + VAT is the maximum potential contract value**, not an initial award. [Challenge 12.4](https://www.civtech.scot/civtech-12-challenge-4-improving-efficiency-and-patient-experience-in-nhs-transport).

Selected exploration teams receive **£5,000**; teams advancing to the accelerator receive **£40,000**. [Round 12 funding explanation](https://www.civtech.scot/blog/get-ready-for-civtech-round-12).

Global individuals/teams/startups can apply; incorporation is required for accelerator selection. No equity or IP ownership transfer, but the sponsor receives a perpetual royalty-free product license. Mostly remote, with required Edinburgh events and potentially sponsor visits. Exploration is paid at its end; accelerator payments occur in five progress-dependent tranches. Further commercial development is not guaranteed. [Official applicant FAQ](https://www.civtech.scot/company-faqs).

**Fit:** low for the current Curbwise site. Patient booking, transport capacity and scheduling are substantially different from street-concept engagement. Only pursue if the founders deliberately want that business and can supply domain/integration expertise. None of the [six live challenges](https://www.civtech.scot/civtech-12-challenges) is a straightforward street-design community pilot.
