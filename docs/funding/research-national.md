# Curbwise funding research: national and replication cities

Checked September 14, 2026. All eligibility and availability claims below use the funders' own current pages. Suggested project framing and judgments about fit are our analysis, not funder approval. No applications or outreach have been sent.

## Recommended order

**Updated after founder direction:** Pilot anywhere with a viable funded scope. Use the [national funding sprint](funding-sprint.md) for current ordering and additional opportunities. The source notes below remain useful. Build local delivery partnerships as part of the funding search; a website being accessible in a city does not by itself establish local benefit.

### 1. Emergent Ventures — direct, open, competitive stretch

- **Status/amount:** Application is live; no deadline shown. The form explicitly says there is no typical award range; funding is project-specific.
- **Applicant/geography:** Individuals age 13+ worldwide; profit from the project is permitted.
- **Application:** Up to 1,500 words covering personal background, a consensus belief, the idea, and a ballpark budget. [Official application](https://mercatus.tfaforms.net/5099527).
- **Use/fit:** Funds must serve charitable, scientific, or educational purposes; lobbying and political activities are prohibited. Our assessment: a demonstrably unusual, scalable tool for residents to understand street-design tradeoffs could fit. Paid development and pilot delivery are plausible requests, subject to grant terms. Show why the project produces better civic decisions, measurable participation, and a route to revenue self-sufficiency. Avoid an undifferentiated “AI renders streets” pitch.
- [Program overview](https://www.mercatus.org/emergent-ventures) confirms its focus on scalable new ideas improving society.

### 2. Awesome Foundation — small direct grants; choose the right chapter

- **Amount/applicant:** Usually $1,000. Individuals, informal groups, and organizations can apply; chapters are independent. [Global FAQ](https://www.awesomefoundation.org/faq/).
- **Chicago:** Active monthly awards; $1,000 for Chicagoland community projects. Applications are reviewed across the 15th–14th window, with decisions on the fourth Tuesday; September 22 and October 27 are listed 2026 meeting dates. [Chicago chapter and application link](https://www.awesomefoundation.org/en/chapters/chicago).
- **SF:** $1,000 monthly; submissions from one calendar month are considered the next. Prefers individuals and public, participatory, playful experiments in its loosely defined region. Profit-centered businesses are excluded. [SF chapter and application link](https://www.awesomefoundation.org/chapters/sf).
- **Our fit assessment:** A “reimagine this block” public workshop with a printed before/after exhibit is stronger than a request for indefinite hosting. Use an actual local organizer for each city. These are pilot grants, too small to sustain operations alone.

### 3. AARP Community Challenge — strong next-cycle pilot target

- **Status:** 2026 closed March 4; 2027 dates are not yet announced. [Current program hub](https://www.aarp.org/livable-communities/community-challenge//).
- **2026 amounts:** $2,500 walk/bike-audit microgrants; flagship grants from a few hundred dollars to $15,000; demonstration awards typically $10,000–$20,000, maximum $25,000.
- **Eligibility:** 501(c)(3)/(4)/(6) organizations and government; other organizations considered individually. Individuals and for-profit companies are excluded. Projects must serve people age 50+.
- **Critical exclusion:** No venture research/development, promotion of for-profit products, or assessments without tangible engagement.
- **Our fit assessment:** A nonprofit-led older-adult walk audit, resident visualization workshop, and tangible follow-up action is credible. A standalone software build is unsuitable. Any platform costs need explicit approval as necessary pilot delivery expenses. [2026 rules](https://www.aarp.org/livable-communities/community-challenge/info-2026/2026-challenge.html).

### 4. America Walks Community Change Grants — next-cycle watch

- **Status:** The program now lists 2026 recipients; no new application deadline is announced. Treat the January application announcement as historical. [Current program page](https://americawalks.org/programs/community-change-grants/).
- **2026 amounts:** Six $2,000 grants; 15 additional $2,200 GM grants for eligible projects involving GM employees or locations with primary GM facilities.
- **Applicant/geography:** U.S. community champions, organizations, and agencies; exact next-cycle legal-entity requirements remain to be checked when released.
- **Our fit assessment:** Strong for an inclusive neighborhood walking/engagement project, weak for general platform operations. Propose a specific neighborhood, new participants, an accessible walk/design activity, and observable follow-through. The program explicitly emphasizes community-wide engagement and increased activity/active transportation. [2026 announcement](https://americawalks.org/apply-for-the-2026-community-change-grant-program/).

### 5. Chicago Community Trust Sponsorships — live, local-partner route

- **Status:** Deadline September 21, 2026, for events in November/December 2026 or January 2027; disbursement November. Award amount is not published on the public page.
- **Applicant:** Chicago/Cook County-serving 501(c)(3), fiscally sponsored organization, or government. Fiscal sponsor must certify a GrantCentral profile before applying. Priority includes budgets below $1 million and organizations without an existing Trust grant.
- **Use:** Event sponsorship; no individual grants, lobbying, or political activity.
- **Our fit assessment:** Pursue a Chicago host in parallel with drafting; the eligible partner and event must be confirmed before submission. Frame safe access to jobs, transit, and neighborhood opportunity with resident-led dialogue; general software operations are a poor fit. The September 21 deadline leaves a short partner-development window. [Current rules and GrantCentral link](https://www.cct.org/grants/opportunities/sponsorships/).

### 6. LA2050 Grants Challenge — local benefit, next-cycle watch

- **Status:** 2026 Part 1 closed April 22; invited Part 2 closed June 5. No 2027 dates verified. [Current FAQ](https://la2050.org/grants-challenge/about).
- **Amount/eligibility:** 2026 awards up to $75,000. Organizer invites nonprofits, social enterprises, and government agencies serving Los Angeles County; attendance at an information session is required. [2026 process](https://la2050.org/blog/new-for-2026-two-part-la2050-grants-challenge-application-process).
- **Our fit assessment:** A credible LA delivery partner and evidence of LA resident benefit are prerequisites for our strategy. Budget eligibility for core software/ongoing operations is not established by the pages reviewed. Consider a defined accessible transportation or civic-participation program if it matches the next cycle's voter-selected issues. Being open nationally on the internet is not enough.

### 7. San Francisco Community Challenge Grants FY27 — live, partner-led physical project

- **Status:** Due October 20, 2026, 2 p.m. local; questions due September 25. Award notifications January 2027; anticipated spring 2027 start. [Current RFP and online application](https://www.sf.gov/community-challenge-grants-request-for-proposals).
- **Amount/applicant:** Requests $50,000–$150,000; 501(c)(3) operating at least two years or qualified fiscal sponsor, SF project, project manager, site contact, and no FY26 award for the applicant. Sponsor MOU required.
- **Use/fit:** Neighborhood beautification, stewardship, events with visible beautification, and gathering spaces. Major construction/public works are excluded. Our assessment: Curbwise could be an engagement component in a locally led placemaking project; a website alone does not meet the scope. First-time City grantees may need cash to cover reimbursable expenses. [FY27 guidebook](https://api.sf.gov/documents/61358/FY27_CCG_Guidebook.pdf).
- The [September 1 addendum](https://www.sf.gov/documents/62728/FY27_CCG_Addendum_3.pdf) says the named fiscal sponsor generally cannot change after application. Review all RFP addenda before drafting. Official RFP/PDF were readable via direct download even where browser search retrieval was blocked.

## Avoid these common false leads

- **PeopleForBikes Industry Community Grants:** Invitation-only; not an open application path. [Current program](https://www.peopleforbikes.org/grants/finder).
- **Roddenberry Catalyst Fund:** Current application FAQ says no grant programs are open and no opening dates are scheduled. Older pages describing rolling grants are not current availability. [Official FAQ](https://apply.roddenberryfoundation.org/app/main/faqs).
- **NLnet:** Active calls exist, but open licensing, European-dimension requirements for EU funds, and AI restrictions make current Curbwise a poor fit without a different independently useful open-source technical scope. Do not force-fit an application. [Current funding criteria](https://nlnet.nl/funding.html), [application requirements](https://nlnet.nl/propose/).
- **SF Foundation Rapid Response Fund:** Requires an unanticipated urgent external event; excludes ongoing programming and general operations. It is unsuitable for planned site launch or normal funding gaps. [Official criteria](https://sff.org/what-we-do/funding/rrf/).

## Application preparation implications

1. Keep two truthful budgets: reusable product/operations and a specific local public-benefit pilot. Local grants should pay only their approved scope; do not disguise R&D as outreach.
2. For each replication city, secure a delivery partner, named community, resident recruitment plan, and a local follow-up decision or action before applying.
3. Keep evidence of turnout, repeat participation, participant feedback, proposal quality, partner use, and actual public follow-up. Do not promise a crash reduction without an evaluation capable of measuring it.
4. For operations, combine a flexible founder grant with paid partner pilots, sponsorship, and donations where an eligible charitable arrangement exists. Restricted microgrants are useful proof and community launch money, not a durable hosting/payroll model.
