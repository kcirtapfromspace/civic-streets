# Fund and launch Curbwise

**Decision brief — September 14, 2026**

> **Updated direction:** The founder has authorized a pilot anywhere that offers a viable funding path. The [national funding sprint](funding-sprint.md) supersedes this document's Denver-first ordering. The budgets, community operating model, and product readiness work below remain useful; Denver is one candidate.

## Recommendation

Choose the first resident-led pilot based on available funding, a willing local host, and affordable delivery. Pursue local project grants alongside requests tailored to software and operations. Build a six-month funding target of **$30,000**, then test paid partner work to sustain the service. Denver remains a prepared option; no city has priority over a better qualified funding route.

The strongest pitch is: **Curbwise helps neighbors turn street concerns into understandable concepts, shared priorities, and documented next steps.** The project already has a substantial software prototype. Its value still needs to be demonstrated with residents and real recipients of the work.

**Assumptions awaiting founder input:** legal structure, first neighborhood, existing relationships, production URL, available time, and actual monthly expenses. Denver is the recommended starting point, not a confirmed location. No grant, sponsor, or partner is committed.

**Potential partner:** Denver Streets Partnership already offers street advocacy resources and community participation opportunities. Ask whether it wants to co-design a pilot or introduce a neighborhood group; do not imply it has endorsed Curbwise. [Get involved](https://denverstreetspartnership.org/get-involved/).

**Potential fiscal sponsor:** Colorado Nonprofit Development Center provides an inquiry route, typically followed by a four-to-six-week full approval process. Its usual model brings projects under its legal/corporate structure. Confirm fees, insurance, governance, and software ownership. It does not supply funding merely by sponsoring a project. [Services and process](https://cndc.org/partner-with-cndc/services/), [inquiry eligibility](https://cndc.org/partner-with-cndc/next-steps/).

An existing willing partner is the more practical path for a near-term deadline. Starting a new nonprofit or sponsorship arrangement solely to chase October funding could consume the entire application window.

## 4. What must operate before public launch

The [source-based readiness review](pilot-readiness.md) found that Denver reports are blocked by Chicago-only geographic validation. It also found incomplete persistent comments/proposals, identity work, submission errors that close the form, and unverified city handoff. Production deployment and existing usage were not inspected.

The first release should let a resident **submit a local concern, reliably retrieve it, explore a concept with a facilitator, and receive follow-up**. A public discussion feed can wait if moderation and durable storage are not ready.

Acceptance conditions:

1. Denver submissions persist and appear on a second device; rejected submissions explain why and preserve the draft.
2. A deployed URL uses real data, supports recovery, limits usage costs, and has an identified operator.
3. Staff identity, report ownership, uploads, moderation, and participant data handling are ready for public use.
4. A resident can participate on mobile, by keyboard, and through assisted/offline intake; needed language support exists.
5. A real recipient and a documented delivery method replace any unverified claim of city integration.

See the readiness review for the eight detailed gates and file references. These are planned fixes, not changes made in this funding-research task.

## 5. Community operating model

**Recruit → contribute → compare ideas → deliver a brief → report back → return.**

Start with a neighborhood issue people already care about. Recruit through trusted local organizations and physical gatherings. Offer a useful first action within ten minutes: document one concern or explain a preference. Invite participants to a scheduled design session and show what changed because of their input.

**People:** one accountable program lead, three resident co-leads, one technical operator, and a named moderator/back-up. Roles may overlap in a small pilot, but their hours and responsibilities must be explicit. Seek resident participation across ages, disabilities, language needs, and travel modes; avoid selecting participants only from existing cycling/urbanist networks.

**Weekly cadence:**

- Review new contributions at least twice weekly; acknowledge within two working days as a proposed service target.
- Hold one short office hour or resident check-in; help people turn concerns into specific observations.
- Send an opt-in weekly digest with one useful update, one upcoming activity, and one next action.
- Publish a monthly “you raised / response so far / next step” brief, including unanswered requests.
- Pay or otherwise explicitly resource resident coordination and access work. Avoid assuming volunteers provide an unlimited service.

Begin with a few authentic example locations contributed with permission. Clearly label demonstration records. Keep policy discussion constructive, explain moderation decisions, and record why residents disagree. Votes are useful signals, not proof of neighborhood-wide consent.

### Proposed first 90-day measures

| Measure | Target / definition |
| --- | --- |
| Distinct participants | 30–50 residents; deduplicate across sessions |
| Useful location records | 20 with location, observation, and context |
| Reviewed priorities | 5, with reasons and differing views recorded |
| Workshops and brief | 2 workshops; 1 delivered community brief |
| Repeat participation | At least 30% of first-month contributors take a second substantive action within 30 days |
| Responsiveness | At least 90% of new contributions receive acknowledgment within two working days |
| Recipient follow-through | Log delivery, acknowledgment, response, and next step separately; no promised government response |

These are targets for the larger pilot, not existing traction. The microgrant version has smaller targets in the application kit. Crash reduction, completed construction, and representative public opinion are not valid short-pilot success claims.

## 7. Expansion and publicity

Choose the next city based on a real host, resident demand, and a recipient for the output. Chicago's existing code does not prove local adoption; LA and SF need independently verified data, participation, and handoff arrangements. Reuse the facilitation kit and software, then localize the community work.

A useful media story follows the first completed loop: residents identified a problem, compared ideas, delivered a brief, and can describe what happened next. Prepare a consented resident story, real before/after concept, accurate participation counts, demo, and a clear invitation to the next activity. Coverage should lead to a staffed event or useful action people can take immediately.
