### Short description

Curbwise proposes a resident-led walk-and-design pilot in [neighborhood]. Neighbors will identify barriers to walking, rolling, biking, and reaching transit; discuss possible improvements; and prepare a short, illustrated brief for local decision-makers. The project combines two neighborhood audits with one hands-on workshop. A facilitator will use Curbwise's existing street-concept and PDF tools to help participants compare ideas and understand tradeoffs. Printed materials and assisted participation will make the activities available to people who do not use the website. Our initial targets are 25 distinct residents, 10 documented observations, three discussion concepts, and one report-back meeting or update. The project will be free to participants.

### Need and local leadership

Residents need a practical way to turn everyday experiences of their streets into specific, understandable requests. This project starts with [community co-lead]'s priorities for [location], which will be documented through [confirmed listening session or existing community evidence]. Residents will choose which locations deserve attention and help interpret the findings. Outreach will include people who walk, use mobility aids, ride transit, bike, and drive, including neighbors who are rarely represented in public meetings.

**Before submission:** Replace the preceding local-evidence fields with actual resident input. Do not claim that a neighborhood requested this pilot until residents have done so.

### Activities and deliverables

1. Recruit a resident co-lead and confirm venue, accessible route, participant notices, and event responsibilities.
2. Conduct two small walk/roll audits, with an indoor or remote participation option. Capture location, observed barrier, and resident explanation; photograph places with permission.
3. Facilitate one workshop comparing three street concepts. Explain dimensional assumptions and record competing priorities, including loading, parking, access, and transit needs.
4. Produce an accessible brief with 10 observations, three concepts, and three resident-selected requests. Identify a real recipient, document delivery, and record any acknowledgment or response.
5. Share what was heard, what was delivered, and what happens next. Invite residents to stay involved through a separate, voluntary follow-up list.

The current software is a prototype. Initial activities can use facilitator-generated PDFs and paper input while Denver reporting is verified. Illustrations support discussion; they do not establish engineering approval or guarantee construction. The project makes no claim that automated city submission or persistent public discussion is already operational.

### Participation and evaluation

Record distinct participants across sessions without publicly identifying them, the number of useful observations, attendance at follow-up, and delivery/acknowledgment of the final brief. Ask participants whether they better understand street-design choices and the next step for their concern. Keep a short record of what participants changed in the proposal. Publish an aggregate summary; participation counts and votes will not be described as representative neighborhood polling.

## 6. Candidate software-funding concept

**Working ask:** $30,000 for a six-month Curbwise community-pilot and product-validation project. This is an applicant-proposed amount; it is not a published award tier. [Emergent Ventures](research-national.md) is one candidate to evaluate against its own purpose and application rules.

**Emergent Ventures version:** Its application permits only charitable, scientific, or educational use and prohibits lobbying and political activity. Limit that request to eligible educational product testing and participation research. Do not transfer the broader kit's policy-organizing activities into this budget; obtain a funder determination for ambiguous outreach. The operating reserve in the general plan requires another unrestricted source or an expressly approved revised allocation. [Application certification](https://mercatus.tfaforms.net/5099527).

**Core argument:** Many residents can explain what feels difficult about their street but have no simple way to compare specific design choices and carry the discussion into a usable document. Curbwise's hypothesis is that accessible concept tools, combined with a small amount of facilitation and visible follow-through, can improve the usefulness of public participation. The existing prototype includes editable street cross-sections, selected dimensional guidance checks, and PDF output. The grant would fund the operating and product work needed to test that hypothesis with real participants.

**Experiment:** Start with one Denver neighborhood partner. Verify persistent local reporting, publish reliable concept/report workflows, and provide assisted participation. During the first 90 days, target 30–50 residents, 20 documented locations, five reviewed priorities, two workshops, and one partner briefing. Over six months, refine the workflow, measure repeat participation and staff time per useful brief, and prepare a reusable facilitation kit. Replicate in a second neighborhood only if residents return and a local recipient uses the output.

**What is uncertain:** We have not established demand, production reliability, a repeatable acquisition channel, willingness to pay, or city uptake. We will test whether people can complete a useful contribution, whether participants return, and whether partners find the brief actionable. We will publish what failed as well as what worked. The project does not promise crash reductions, engineering certification, or automated approval of designs.

**Sustainability hypothesis:** Keep the community pilot free, and test whether organizations will pay for facilitation, reporting, and continuing service. Sponsor support can underwrite access. These are proposals to validate, not current revenue or committed partners. Tailor any application to permitted educational/research activities and funder restrictions.

**Supporting material to add:** Founder biography and relevant work, real demo URL/video, honest product screenshots, confirmed partner letter, local listening evidence, cost quotations, and the [six-month budget](funding-plan.md). The eventual application should explain the specific original contribution after checking comparable tools; do not claim Curbwise has no competitors. For Emergent Ventures, add the applicant's own answer about a consensus belief, project history, and time commitment. Its form does not accept PDF attachments; use accepted formats or the multimedia-link field.
