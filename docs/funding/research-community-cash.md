# Additional community cash opportunities

**Verified September 14, 2026.** Scope: grants for resident organizing, street-safety activities, and public workshops. No outreach or submissions made. Founder/entity status is unknown. Amounts below are published award ranges or caps; they are not guaranteed awards or expected revenue. Geographic expansion requires an actual local team and activity.

## Recommended order

1. **Pollination Project:** smallest paperwork/entity barrier; appropriate for a $500 volunteer-run kickoff.
2. **Walmart Spark Good:** apply through an existing eligible community partner in the real service area; seek a concrete $1,000–$2,500 workshop budget before chasing the $5,000 ceiling.
3. **New England Grassroots Seed:** worthwhile with two genuine local organizers and an action-oriented pilot.
4. **Seattle civic education or school partner:** strong subject fit, with grant-contract/cash-flow checks first.
5. **Sustainable CT:** useful if a local team can raise donations; it doubles raised money rather than supplying unrestricted startup capital.

These rankings and request sizes are our judgment. Most of these programs buy public activities and supplies; they do not offer founder living expenses or unrestricted commercial software runway.

## 1. The Pollination Project — global, rolling

**Award:** up to **$500**, one-time. Individuals, informal groups, and nonprofits worldwide can apply. Organization budget must be below $50,000; total project below $10,000; no paid staff; some work must already have started. Prior TPP recipients, including people in the same group, cannot obtain another seed award. Occasional outside professional contracting is allowed. [Current application guidance](https://thepollinationproject.org/apply/)

**Cash timing:** Current application page says complete September applications are reviewed in October, with decisions around month-end. A separate official FAQ still says **6–12 weeks**, so budget conservatively; payout timing after selection is not specified. USD PayPal or wire receipt is required. [Application timeline](https://thepollinationproject.org/apply/), [other official FAQ](https://thepollinationproject.org/frequently-asked-questions-about-our-grants/)

**Costs/restrictions:** No match or application fee is listed. Cannot pay the applicant/family for work. No animal products at funded events. Receipts, photos/video, reporting, and publicity participation are required. [Grant conditions](https://thepollinationproject.org/apply/)

**Curbwise pitch:** One free, volunteer-led walk/roll audit and street-design session; printing, accessibility materials, supplies, and plant-based refreshments. This is a plausible fit, subject to actual volunteer/entity eligibility. [Start application](https://thepollinationproject.smapply.us/)

## 2. New England Grassroots Environment Fund — Seed

**Award:** **$500–$1,000**; rolling, with no January/July reviews. Work must occur in **CT, ME, MA, NH, RI, or VT**. At least **two organizers** must share decisions. Formal nonprofit status and fiscal sponsorship are unnecessary. A group-named bank account is needed for payment, but can be arranged after application. Volunteer groups and budgets below $100,000 are prioritized; groups above $150,000 are ineligible. [Seed rules and application](https://grassrootsfund.org/grant-programs/seed-grants)

**Cash timing:** Usually **4–6 weeks** to decision, then **2–3 weeks after completed paperwork**. No match or application charge is listed; any chosen sponsor/bank fee must be established separately. No for-profit business expenses, lobbying, national work, retroactive costs, stand-alone studies, or report publication. Only one application can be under review. [Seed rules](https://grassrootsfund.org/grant-programs/seed-grants)

**Curbwise pitch:** A resident-led mobility/accessibility group organizing accessible street walks, participatory design sessions, and concrete follow-up action. A website rollout or report-only project is weak. Request $1,000 against direct local activities, with residents controlling priorities.

## 3. Walmart Spark Good Local Grants — nationwide local facilities

**Award:** **$250–$5,000** cash. The current **FY27 Cycle 3 is August 1–November 30, 2026**. Project must serve the same area as the Walmart, Sam’s Club, or distribution facility receiving the request. Applicant needs a Spark Good account and **Deed verification**. [Current guidelines](https://www.walmart.org/how-we-give/program-guidelines/spark-good-local-grants-guidelines)

Eligible applicants include 501(c)(3) public charities, recognized government entities, qualifying schools, and faith organizations with community-wide projects. An individual or ordinary for-profit founder cannot directly qualify. A willing local nonprofit can lead the program and application; confirm any vendor or fiscal-sponsorship arrangement with that partner. [Eligibility FAQ](https://www.walmart.org/how-we-give/faqs)

**Cash timing:** Decisions are made before the next cycle opens; this is not an immediate-cash promise. Recommended grantees must provide banking through JPMorgan within **21 days** of notification. No match/application fee is listed. Logo/recognition conditions apply. Maximum 25 pending/approved applications; each must genuinely benefit the facility’s area, and no repeat award from the same facility that fiscal year. [Guidelines](https://www.walmart.org/how-we-give/program-guidelines/spark-good-local-grants-guidelines)

**Curbwise pitch:** Free local safety/accessibility workshops with a partner hosting, recruiting, and reporting results. Published examples include awards of $250, $500, $1,000, $1,500, and $2,000; the $5,000 cap is not a typical-award estimate. [Examples](https://www.walmart.org/how-we-give/program-guidelines/spark-good-local-grants-guidelines)

## 4. Seattle Small Sparks — Government 101 inquiry, conditional availability

**Verification limit:** The current city page offers an eligibility conversation for this track. An actually accepting application form and remaining balance were not verified; keep this in the inquiry queue, outside confirmed application-ready opportunities.

**Award:** up to **$5,000** for Seattle civic education. **General Small Sparks has closed**, but the current program page directs civic-education/local-government projects to the Government 101 track and a project-manager eligibility conversation. The track announcement lists rolling applications until **October 30, 2026 or exhaustion**, submitted at least six weeks before activities. Neighborhood, community, informal, and business groups are listed as eligible. [Current availability](https://seattle.gov/neighborhoods/community-grants/neighborhood-matching-fund), [Government 101 announcement](https://frontporch.seattle.gov/2026/04/09/turn-curiosity-into-civic-power-get-up-to-5000-for-community-led-projects/)

**Cash timing/costs:** Decisions within **six weeks**, then contracts within **four weeks** after award. Community match can include volunteer time, donated materials/services, or cash. Do not assume a cash advance: get written confirmation of match valuation, fiscal-sponsor requirements/fees, insurance, and reimbursement/advance terms before committing expenditure. Current public summary does not specify payout dates. [Program terms](https://seattle.gov/neighborhoods/community-grants/neighborhood-matching-fund)

**Curbwise pitch:** “How to get a safer street”: free accessible workshops teaching residents how Seattle street decisions and budgets work, documenting concerns, and practicing public comment. A software product demo alone is insufficient. Confirm remaining funds with the project manager; no contact has been made.

## 5. Seattle Safe Routes to School Mini Grants

**Award:** up to **$1,500**, one grant per applicant per year; **rolling**. Eligible: Seattle public/private K–12 schools, PTAs, and other nonprofit groups. Requires a **principal’s support letter** identifying the activity and applicant role. The official September 2, 2026 update explicitly invites applications. [Current mini-grant page](https://seattle.gov/transportation/projects-and-programs/safety-first/safe-routes-to-school/mini-grants), [September 2026 confirmation](https://sdotblog.seattle.gov/2026/09/02/safety-projects-support-children-walking-biking-to-school/)

**Cash timing/costs:** Coordinator response expected within **one month**; this is a receipt/response target, not a guaranteed award or payout date. No match or application fee is stated. Confirm payment mechanism and any partner administration costs. Reporting due one year after award. Bicycles cannot be purchased. [Application requirements](https://seattle.gov/transportation/projects-and-programs/safety-first/safe-routes-to-school/mini-grants)

**Curbwise pitch:** A school/PTA-led walk/roll-to-school event and family route workshop using the map to record specific barriers, with safety/event supplies. Existing examples include bike rodeos, intersection murals, event supplies, and outdoor clothing. Curbwise participates as a tool/facilitator; the school community owns the activity. Hardware construction requires separate permissions.

## 6. Sustainable CT Community Match Fund

**Award:** dollar-for-dollar crowdfunding match **up to $6,000**, with no minimum budget; rolling/no deadline. Project must offer a **free public benefit in a Sustainable CT registered Connecticut town**. Individuals, community groups, municipalities, schools, libraries, and eligible nonprofits can lead. Staff approve project/readiness, then a **30–60-day** Patronicity campaign runs; funds are disbursed after the successful campaign. No fixed approval or bank-arrival guarantee. [Current program](https://sustainablect.org/funding)

**Important eligibility/cost limits:** For-profits are excluded; nonprofit annual budget cannot exceed $300,000. No organizational overhead, existing staff, or paying yourself. Equipment/infrastructure purchases require nonprofit leadership. Sponsor may be a nonprofit or municipality; only sponsor administration fees are permitted. Campaigns need donor-count participation equal to the fundraising goal divided by 100 (a $3,000 goal requires at least 30 donations). Large single gifts and self-donations have matching caps. Prior-raised funds do not count. [Currently linked eligibility guide](https://sustainablect.org/fileadmin/Random_PDF_Files/Files_and_Resources/2026_Community_Match_Fund_Eligibility_Requirements.pdf)

Patronicity’s standard fee is **5% of funds raised plus 2.6% + $0.30 per online contribution**; donors can cover fees. Confirm program-specific fee treatment, including match dollars, in the approved budget. Fiscal sponsorship and donation tax treatment are not automatically supplied by using the platform. [Patronicity FAQ](https://www.patronicity.com/faq)

**Curbwise pitch:** Public accessibility/bike-safety workshops and community action hosted by a local team. A $3,000 campaign plus $3,000 match is a **$6,000 gross project**, before fees; it is not a $6,000 grant. Do not move here solely for this capped opportunity.

## 7. Deadline tomorrow: New England Grassroots Grow

**Open Fall 2026 application deadline: September 15. Award: $1,000–$4,000**, with funder encouraging $4,000 requests where usable. This serves **established local groups** with experience and shared community decision-making in New England. It is a stronger option for an already-active local partner than a newly invented affiliate. [Current Grow application and eligibility](https://grassrootsfund.org/grant-programs/grow-grants)

**Not fast cash:** Fall decisions typically arrive in **early January**, paperwork follows in **3–4 weeks**, and funds in **2–3 weeks after completed paperwork**. One active Seed/Grow application at a time. Choose Seed for new organizing; Grow only when the group's actual history supports it. No match/application fee is listed; receiving-account/sponsor arrangements still matter. [Grow timeline](https://grassrootsfund.org/grant-programs/grow-grants)

## Application package to reuse

Prepare a 150-word local need statement, named resident co-leaders, a two-event agenda, a line-item budget, real venue/partner commitments, accessibility provisions, and three outputs: participants served, priority locations reviewed, and a documented follow-up meeting. Show Curbwise as existing project infrastructure; fund the public work. Track each award against separate eligible expenses and disclose other funding when requested. No claimed partner, geography, nonprofit status, or prior outcomes should be invented to qualify.
