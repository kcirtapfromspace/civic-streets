# Five prospective community hosts

**Checked September 14, 2026. No contact made.** These organizations have relevant public programs and contact paths. Hosting, fiscal sponsorship, grant eligibility, budgets, and willingness remain unconfirmed. Suggested roles are our assessment, not commitments. A larger statewide organization may be more useful as a connector to resident groups than as applicant for a small-budget grant.

## 1. Denver Streets Partnership — Denver, Colorado

**Why approach:** Its public involvement work includes community events, Denver Streets Congress discussions about health/accessibility, and a “Fix My Street!” advocacy guide. These align with documenting resident concerns and turning them into specific requests. [Programs](https://denverstreetspartnership.org/get-involved/)

**Proposed role:** Identify one neighborhood and co-design a walk/roll audit plus concept workshop; help determine the right city recipient and local grant route. Begin with the existing resident need and offer Curbwise as supporting infrastructure.

**Public contact:** [Contact form](https://denverstreetspartnership.org/get-involved/contact/). The official form accepts name, email, and message; no named staff relationship is assumed.

## 2. WalkMassachusetts — Boston, Massachusetts; statewide reach

**Why approach:** Its Walk Audit Academy explicitly treats audits as community engagement and offers cohort training for three to five local groups. That is close to the proposed workshop method. [Walk Audit Academy](https://walkmass.org/walk-audit-academy/)

**Proposed role:** Methodology reviewer and referral partner for a Massachusetts resident group; explore a small digital documentation/design session alongside existing audit training. Ask how Curbwise could complement its existing audit dashboard rather than duplicating its work.

**Public contact:** contact-us@walkmass.org; 617-367-9255. [Current contact page](https://walkmass.org/contact-us/)

## 3. Seattle Streets Alliance — Seattle, Washington

**Current name:** Formerly Seattle Neighborhood Greenways. Its current participation page lists neighborhood organizing, walks/rides, mapping, research, grantwriting, and annual resident priorities. [Ways to participate](https://www.streetsalliance.org/get-involved/)

**Proposed role:** Refer a neighborhood group or school-safety organizer for a free “how street decisions work” workshop. Explore Government 101 eligibility or a school/PTA activity only after the host and city confirm fit. Ask for a local co-leader rather than assuming citywide sponsorship.

**Public contact:** info@streetsalliance.org; [contact page and neighborhood-group finder](https://www.streetsalliance.org/who-we-are/contact-us/). Use the current domain rather than older Greenways contact details.

## 4. Active Transportation Alliance — Chicago, Illinois

**Why approach:** It works with residents and community leaders on walking, biking, and transit and advertises Advocacy Connect conversations for city/suburban advocates. [Current organization/program page](https://activetransportation.org/)

**Proposed role:** Introduce a neighborhood mobility group, review a workshop agenda, or help direct a concrete project toward an appropriate local funder. Chicago also matches the prototype’s current report boundary, although operational readiness still needs verification.

**Public contact:** 312-427-3325, published on the [main contact footer](https://activetransportation.org/). Its [official event-system contact page](https://organizations.activetransportation.org/sites/organization_sites/fd0d3f3754acb0b0fd0e/pages/contact_us) lists info@activetransportation.org.

## 5. Bike Walk Connecticut — West Hartford, Connecticut; statewide reach

**Why approach:** Its participation page describes education, safety workshops, community events, and partnerships supporting connected walking/biking routes. [Program fit](https://bikewalkct.org/get-involved/)

**Proposed role:** Connect a resident-led team in a Sustainable CT registered town, help select a practical workshop/safety activity, and assess whether a matching campaign can attract local donors. Do not assume the organization itself satisfies any funder budget cap.

**Public contact:** BikeWalkCT@BikeWalkCT.org; 860-288-2543. Its [contact form](https://bikewalkct.org/contact/) explicitly includes “Running an Event” and “Creating a Partnership” options.

## First conversation objective

Seek one named co-leader, one real local problem, a tentative venue/date, and agreement on resident recruitment and decision-maker follow-up. Then confirm the applicant, permitted expenses, and any host fee before completing a grant budget. Offer a short prototype demo and two-session outline; do not describe an organization as a partner until it agrees.
