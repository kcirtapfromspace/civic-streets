## Claims the repository supports

Suggested application language:

> Curbwise is a working software prototype that helps residents document street concerns and explore street improvement concepts. Its existing code includes a map interface, categorized reports with photos, editable street cross-sections, before-and-after views, and PDF generation. The proposed pilot will adapt and verify these workflows for one Colorado neighborhood, support residents through facilitated activities, and produce a documented package of community priorities for local decision-makers.

## Pilot participation funnel

**Recruit → contribute → deliberate → hand off → report back → return.** Begin with one neighborhood partner and one bounded area. Seek residents who walk, roll, bike, ride transit, and drive; deliberately include people who seldom attend planning meetings. Do not interpret votes as a representative survey.

1. **Recruit:** Partner invitations, printed QR cards, and an accessible kickoff/walk audit. Measure invitations and attendance; collect separate permission for follow-up.
2. **Contribute:** Help each resident document one concern or support an existing concern. Measure distinct contributors, completed reports, and time to first successful contribution. Keep an assisted paper option.
3. **Deliberate:** Hold two facilitated design sessions using the working cross-section/PDF tools. Record the reasons behind priorities, access needs, and disagreements; capture only necessary participant information.
4. **Hand off:** Produce a small, reviewed package of priority locations, evidence, concepts, and requested next steps. Acknowledge receipt with the named city or community partner. Measure receipt and response, separately from repairs.
5. **Report back:** Publish a monthly “you raised / partner response / next step” update. Invite contributors to the next session and measure return participation.

For a 90-day pilot, proposed targets could be 30–50 participants, 20 documented locations, 5 reviewed priorities, 2 workshops, and 1 partner briefing. These are planning targets, not traction claims. Track repeat participation and partner acknowledgment; do not promise infrastructure construction or statistically measurable crash reductions within this period. Expansion to Chicago, Los Angeles, or San Francisco should follow evidence that this loop works locally, with a separate local partner and verified data/intake arrangements in each city.
